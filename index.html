Popular Brewing Methods

Discover different techniques to extract the perfect flavor from your coffee beans

French Press

A classic immersion brewing method that produces rich, full-bodied coffee. Perfect for those who enjoy a robust flavor profile with natural oils and sediment that add depth to every sip. Brew time: 4-5 minutes.

Steps to Brew

  1. Add coffee and hot water (≈ 93 °C).
  2. Stir gently, ensuring all grounds are wet.
  3. Cover and steep 4 minutes.
  4. Press slowly and pour immediately.

Espresso

The foundation of café culture. High-pressure extraction creates concentrated, intense shots with a signature crema. The base for lattes, cappuccinos, and countless specialty drinks. Extraction time: 25-30 seconds.

Steps to Brew

  1. Grind fine (like powdered sugar).
  2. Tamp evenly with about 30 lb pressure.
  3. Start shot immediately after locking in.
  4. Stop when 36 g is reached (≈ 25–30 s).

Moka Pot

A precise, meditative brewing method that highlights coffee's subtle flavors and aromatics. Control water temperature and pour rate to extract a clean, nuanced cup. Total brew time: 3-4 minutes.

Steps to Brew

  1. Fill bottom chamber with hot water below safety valve.
  2. Add finely-ground coffee to filter basket and level it.
  3. Assemble and place on low-medium heat.
  4. Wait until gurgling sound begins.
  5. Remove from heat once top chamber is ¾ full.
  6. Swirl and serve.

Pour Over

A precise, meditative brewing method that highlights coffee's subtle flavors and aromatics. Control water temperature and pour rate to extract a clean, nuanced cup. Total brew time: 3-4 minutes.

Steps to Brew

  1. Rinse paper filter and preheat the dripper.
  2. Add grounds; make a small well.
  3. Pour 40 g water to bloom (30 s).
  4. Continue slow circular pours until 300 g.
  5. Let it drain completely, then enjoy.

Cold Brew

A slow, cold extraction method that produces a smooth, mellow coffee concentrate. Ideal for iced coffee drinks. Brew time: 12-24 hours.

Steps to Brew

  1. Combine coffee and water.
  2. Stir to saturate all grounds.
  3. Cover and refrigerate 12–16 hours.
  4. Strain, then store in fridge for up to 1 week.

South India Filter

A precise, meditative brewing method that highlights coffee's subtle flavors and aromatics. Control water temperature and pour rate to extract a clean, nuanced cup. Total brew time: 3-4 minutes.

Steps to Brew

  1. Add 3 tbsp coffee powder to the upper filter chamber.
  2. Place the pressing disc on top and level it.
  3. Pour 150 ml hot water just below boiling.
  4. Let it drip slowly for 10–15 minutes.
  5. Mix ¼ cup decoction with ¾ cup hot milk.
  6. Add sugar as needed and froth by pouring between tumblers.
  7. Serve hot and enjoy.

INVERTED AEROPRESS

A precise, meditative brewing method that highlights coffee's subtle flavors and aromatics. Control water temperature and pour rate to extract a clean, nuanced cup. Total brew time: 3-4 minutes.

Steps to Brew

  1. Assemble AeroPress upside down with plunger at the bottom.
  2. Add 16 g coffee and 50 g water to bloom for 30 sec.
  3. Pour remaining water up to the top and stir gently.
  4. Steep for 1 min, then attach cap with filter.
  5. Flip carefully over the cup and press slowly.
  6. Enjoy a clean, full-bodied brew.

CHANNI

A precise, meditative brewing method that highlights coffee's subtle flavors and aromatics. Control water temperature and pour rate to extract a clean, nuanced cup. Total brew time: 3-4 minutes.

Steps to Brew

  1. Boil 1 cup water and add 1–2 tsp coffee powder.
  2. Let it simmer for 2–3 minutes.
  3. Pour through a channi (fine mesh strainer) into a cup.
  4. Add hot milk and sugar to taste.
  5. Stir well and serve immediately.

AEROPRESS

Steps to Brew

  1. Insert and rinse the paper filter.
  2. Add 16 g medium-ground coffee.
  3. Pour 50 g hot water to bloom for 30 sec.
  4. Add remaining water up to 220 g and stir.
  5. Wait 1 min, then press gently for 30 sec.
  6. Enjoy a smooth, balanced cup.

Explore Coffee Varieties

Understanding different coffee species and their unique characteristics

Arabica

The most popular coffee species, known for its sweet, delicate flavor with hints of fruit and sugar. Grown at higher altitudes in Latin America, East Africa, and Asia. Accounts for about 60% of world production. Arabica beans are oval-shaped and have a curved crease.

Robusta

A bold, strong coffee with higher caffeine content and earthy, nutty flavors. More resistant to pests and diseases, primarily grown in Vietnam, Brazil, and Indonesia. Often used in espresso blends for added body and crema. Contains nearly twice the caffeine of Arabica.

Liberica

A rare variety with irregular bean shapes and distinctive smoky, woody flavors with floral notes. Grown primarily in the Philippines and Malaysia. Known for its unique, polarizing taste profile. The beans are larger and more asymmetrical than other species.

Excelsa

Technically a variety of Liberica with tart, fruity flavors that add complexity to blends. Grown in Southeast Asia. Contributes depth and an exotic flavor dimension that enhances coffee's middle and back notes. Represents only a small fraction of global production.

Tips for the Perfect Brew

Expert advice to elevate your coffee-making skills

Use Freshly Roasted Beans

Coffee is best consumed within 2-4 weeks of roasting. Look for roast dates on packaging and buy from local roasters when possible. Store beans in an airtight container away from light, heat, and moisture to preserve freshness.

Grind Size Matters

Match your grind to your brewing method. Coarse for French press, medium for drip coffee makers, and fine for espresso. Invest in a quality burr grinder for consistent results. Grind just before brewing for maximum freshness.

Water Temperature Control

Water temperature should be around 90–96°C (195–205°F). Too hot and you'll extract bitter compounds, too cool and you'll under-extract. Let boiling water rest for 30 seconds before brewing for optimal temperature.

Experiment with Brewing Ratios

Start with a 1:16 coffee-to-water ratio (15g coffee to 240ml water) and adjust to taste. Keep notes on what works best for your palate and beans. A kitchen scale ensures consistency and helps you replicate successful brews.
