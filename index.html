Explore Coffee Varieties

Understanding different coffee species and their unique characteristics

Arabica

The most popular coffee species, known for its sweet, delicate flavor with hints of fruit and sugar. Grown at higher altitudes in Latin America, East Africa, and Asia. Accounts for about 60% of world production. Arabica beans are oval-shaped and have a curved crease.

Robusta

A bold, strong coffee with higher caffeine content and earthy, nutty flavors. More resistant to pests and diseases, primarily grown in Vietnam, Brazil, and Indonesia. Often used in espresso blends for added body and crema. Contains nearly twice the caffeine of Arabica.

Liberica

A rare variety with irregular bean shapes and distinctive smoky, woody flavors with floral notes. Grown primarily in the Philippines and Malaysia. Known for its unique, polarizing taste profile. The beans are larger and more asymmetrical than other species.

Excelsa

Technically a variety of Liberica with tart, fruity flavors that add complexity to blends. Grown in Southeast Asia. Contributes depth and an exotic flavor dimension that enhances coffee's middle and back notes. Represents only a small fraction of global production.

Tips for the Perfect Brew

Expert advice to elevate your coffee-making skills

Use Freshly Roasted Beans

Coffee is best consumed within 2-4 weeks of roasting. Look for roast dates on packaging and buy from local roasters when possible. Store beans in an airtight container away from light, heat, and moisture to preserve freshness.

Grind Size Matters

Match your grind to your brewing method. Coarse for French press, medium for drip coffee makers, and fine for espresso. Invest in a quality burr grinder for consistent results. Grind just before brewing for maximum freshness.

Water Temperature Control

Water temperature should be around 90–96°C (195–205°F). Too hot and you'll extract bitter compounds, too cool and you'll under-extract. Let boiling water rest for 30 seconds before brewing for optimal temperature.

Experiment with Brewing Ratios

Start with a 1:16 coffee-to-water ratio (15g coffee to 240ml water) and adjust to taste. Keep notes on what works best for your palate and beans. A kitchen scale ensures consistency and helps you replicate successful brews.
